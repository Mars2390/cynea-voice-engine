Highlights

Four claims, and the receipts for each

Every number below is a constant you can open in the repository or a recording you can play on this page. Where something is a design target rather than a measurement, it says so.

Voice vendors tend to lead with a latency figure and a benchmark nobody can reproduce. We would rather show you the four things that are actually true today, and say plainly what is still ahead.

  1. 01

    The voices are real people

    Kwame, Amina, Kofi and Maya are four recordings of human beings, not synthesis dressed up as one. Every clip on this page is a slice of tape somebody actually spoke.

    Hear all four →
  2. 02

    Nine synthetic voices when you need words we never recorded

    Four of the nine are African locales — two Nigerian, two South African — alongside British and American. They run on edge-tts, which costs nothing per word.

  3. 03

    Turn-taking with the windows written down

    It waits 700 ms after you stop before it answers, offers a backchannel once a mid-sentence pause passes 800 ms, and only treats you as interrupting at 3 words — so a stray “mhm” never cuts it off.

    Call audio can stay on your own machines

    Whisper and edge-tts both run locally, so recordings never have to cross a vendor boundary to be understood or answered. The model that writes the replies is the one part you choose.

FAQ

Questions, answered

An AI receptionist answers your business line the way a trained front-desk person would — it greets the caller, understands what they want in natural speech, and either resolves it or routes it. In Kenya it runs on a local number provisioned through Africa's Talking, so callers dial an ordinary Kenyan number and pay ordinary local rates.

Transcription handles Swahili through Whisper. On the speech side we ship nine voices today, four of them native African accents (Nigerian and South African). A dedicated Swahili TTS voice is on the roadmap rather than in the product — we would rather say that plainly than let you discover it on a live call.

Seven, via Africa's Talking: Kenya, Nigeria, Ghana, South Africa, Uganda, Tanzania and Rwanda. Anywhere else, bring your own carrier over SIP.

Three steps: provision a Nairobi number (or forward your existing one), pick a persona and edit its prompt, then point the SIP endpoint at Cynea. The agent configuration is a single Python module per persona, so a developer can have a first version answering within a day.

The design target is roughly 600 ms to first audio, achieved by streaming LLM tokens straight into the synthesiser rather than waiting for a finished sentence. Turn-taking deliberately adds a 700 ms grace period after you stop speaking so the agent does not talk over you.

Yes — once, on the opening turn, in its own register, and again any time a caller asks directly. It does not repeat a robotic disclaimer, because that is itself a tell. This is the disclosed-but-natural default and it ships that way for California SB 1001, EU AI Act Article 50 and Kenya ODPC 2024.

Whisper transcribes code-switched speech well, and the personas are written to expect it — Kwame is a Ghanaian hotelier, not a generic assistant. Full Twi and Pidgin synthesis is roadmap, not shipped.

It escalates. The engine watches for complaints and repeated repair attempts, then hands the call to a person with the full transcript, so the caller never has to start over.
